The split-brain lab

Three copies of one board, A, B and C, joined by a simulated network. Click a wire to cut it. Make the network slow and lossy. Edit different copies while they can't see each other. When you reconnect, every copy lands on the same text and the same fingerprint.

Things to try

  1. Same spot, same time. Cut every wire, put the cursor at the end of the note in A and in C, and type different words. Reconnect. The words don't interleave letter by letter.
  2. Delete vs. edit. Isolate A. Throw random edits. Reconnect. Notes deleted in one copy but changed in another follow the paper's add-wins rule.
  3. A terrible network. Push lost and duplicated messages to 60% and latency to 3 seconds. Lost messages are resent, duplicates are ignored, and early arrivals wait for what they depend on. The copies still converge.

The same simulator runs in the test suite, where it checks 3,000 random schedules per property on every build.
